01 The Hypothesis

Prices moved first.
Jobs move next.

A decade ago, economists stopped waiting for official inflation statistics and started scraping millions of online prices themselves. This page asks whether the same bottom-up approach (high-frequency, unofficial, built from what's actually being posted online) can track AI's effect on India's labour market faster and more honestly than static, survey-based exposure maps.

02 The Original Method

What was the Billion Prices Project?

Starting in 2008, Alberto Cavallo and Roberto Rigobon scraped hundreds of millions of prices from online retailers around the world, rebuilding daily inflation estimates from the ground up: no survey, no government office, no lag. In 2011 the method caught Argentina's government publishing an official inflation rate roughly a third of what the scraped data actually showed, turning an academic side-project into one of the sharpest checks on state statistics of the decade.

→

Millions of scraped prices, aggregated daily, form a continuous index. This page applies the same basic shape to job postings, hiring disclosures and AI-usage data instead of prices.

The gender flip

India is the only country in the study where women show lower AI exposure than men, because women are concentrated in agriculture. Every other sampled country shows the opposite.

Data staleness

India's figures come from PLFS 2018-19, before ChatGPT existed, and were coded at a coarser 3-digit occupation level, versus 4-digit for other countries in the same study.

05 The Dynamic Evidence

Job postings already show the shift the survey data can't see yet.

Copestake, Marczinek, Pople & Stapleton track AI-skill demand inside the text of Indian job adverts: near-exponential growth since 2016, concentrated in IT, finance and professional services, with a negative effect on non-AI postings and top-percentile wages in high-skilled, non-routine roles.

Source: Copestake et al., "AI and Services-Led Growth: Evidence from Indian Job Adverts" (2023, upd. 2024–25)

08 What We Can't See

Every proxy here still stops at the edge of the formal economy.

Population-wide administrative payroll data?

Monthly aggregate by industry only, not individual-level
Using 25,000 workers linked to full administrative wage and hours records, we find precise null effects on earnings and hours two years post-ChatGPT (ruling out effects larger than 2%) despite widespread adoption. What moved instead was task composition and occupational mobility.
Humlum & Vestergaard, "Still Waters, Rapid Currents" (NBER / Rockwool, 2025–26)

10 The Verdict

The method works. The data infrastructure doesn't, yet.

  • The method works in principle: Copestake et al. already show job-ad-based tracking detects real AI labour effects inside India.
  • The signal is live and visible right now: Anthropic's own India data plus TCS, Infosys and Xpheno numbers, concentrated in IT and urban India.
  • But India lacks payroll-level data infrastructure: unlike the US and Denmark, so any index has to stay indirect and proxy-based, and wage-effect claims should stay cautious.
